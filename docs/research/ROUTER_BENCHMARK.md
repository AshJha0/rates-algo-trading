# Router benchmark

Generated by `RouterBenchmarkTest`; do not edit by hand. This is a result of the router's own synthetic simulator (`ImpactMarket`), not a market finding: it says how the router behaves in a model whose every number is a declared assumption, against alternatives a desk would actually use. It replaces the 2.94 bp row, which compared the router with orders fired whole into thin touch depth (review section 5.1).

## Scenario

The router is given only what a desk would know: published fees, lot and tick, its measured latency, a daily volume figure and a declared cancellation-rate prior of 0.2 per second (`ImpactConfig.declaredVenues`). Queue cancellation and order arrival rates are learned from trade prints and book decrements; the simulator's own rates are never passed in. A fresh router each day: nothing it learns carries over.

Efficient price: 0.209 ticks per root second (0.5 price points a day); every lot anyone takes moves it 0.02 ticks for good, and the depth taken refills, so resilience is below 100%. Steps of 100 ms, 10 levels a side. Parents: 10 to 60 lots of 1 million, random side, 30 s apart, urgency 0.5; the router's nominal schedule at that urgency is 5 slices over 60 s with 15 s passive, which baselines (b) and (c) follow and (d) uses as its 75 s deadline.

Strategies: the router (ladder model, `RouterPresets.ladder()` with `ExecutionModel.defaults()`); (a) the whole parent swept best all-in price first across venues on arrival; (b) the router's time schedule swept on the single best venue (venue 1); (c) TWAP across venues, each slice swept best price first; (d) resting at the best touch, re-posted when the touch moves away, swept at the deadline; (e) the old single-shot and even-split baselines with an 8-tick collar, labelled strawmen; and the touch-model router (v1 preset) for reference. Sweeps read 10 levels and retry remainders after 0.5 s.

Declared before the run: the router's risk-adjusted cost should be no worse than any of (a) to (d) by more than 0.25 ticks. The one run met that against (a), (b) and (c) and did not against (d) (see the check at the end); the scenario was not changed. The test asserts the check for (a) to (c), that the report records the failure against (d), that every parent completes, that no fill is outside its order's limit, and that the router never prices past its 2-tick collar or crosses itself.

One run. The numbers are deterministic for the seed, so regenerating the file with the command at the end repeats the same run. A first attempt stopped before producing any number (a strawman baseline hung on a rejected order; fixed in 20484e2).

- Against (b) schedule, best venue: the router's risk-adjusted cost is lower, and the interval excludes zero (difference 0.5 ticks, 95% [0.3, 0.7]).

## The check declared before the run

The router's risk-adjusted cost was to be no worse than each real baseline's by more than 0.25 ticks.

- (a) sweep on arrival: router worse by 0.14 ticks: holds.
- (b) schedule, best venue: router worse by -0.47 ticks: holds.
- (c) TWAP across venues: router worse by -0.47 ticks: holds.
- (d) passive, deadline: router worse by 0.40 ticks: FAILS.

The strawmen (single shot on one venue and the even split, both with the old 8-tick collar) are kept only to show what the earlier 2.94 bp claim was measured against.
