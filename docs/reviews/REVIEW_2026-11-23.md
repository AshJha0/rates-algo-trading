# rates-algo-trading: in-depth review, 2026-11-23

Reviewed at `main` 44c4f45 (v1.0.0 plus PRs #7 to #9). Six independent read-only reviews (pricing, research evidence, quoting and risk, SOR and market data, latency and FIX, engineering and site), about 55k lines of Java and the Python research package. Nothing was built, run or changed.

**Evidence classes used below**

- **[C] Confirmed**: I re-read the cited lines myself after the reviewer reported.
- **[R] Reviewer-read**: a reviewer read the code and cited lines; I did not re-read them.
- **[U] Unverified**: from memory or inference; check before acting.

## 1. Verdict

The plumbing is better than most public projects of this kind: deterministic replay, hash-chained journals, allocation-free loops, a real QuantLib-checked pricing library, a pre-registration ledger, and unusually candid limits. Judged against a bank desk, a hedge fund or an HFT firm, it is not tradable, and the published numbers do not mean what a reader will take them to mean. Five things decide that:

1. **Every P&L figure is fixed by declared simulator constants.** The desk's half-spread is capped at 0.08 bp while the simulator charges an expected 0.2 bp of adverse selection per swap trade. The −109k per session is arithmetic, not a finding. The positive figures (2.2mm a day, Sharpe 1.5 to 1.9) depend on clients accepting prices far behind the best quote.
2. **The trading process does not use the pricing library.** Swap fair value is the par rate of the swap that was just fed in; bond fair value is book mid. The desk has no independent view of value.
3. **The safety path fails open.** A dead loop thread leaves live quotes in the market behind a healthy FIX session, and the kill switch travels through that same thread and reports success regardless.
4. **The SOR and latency headlines are measured against strawmen.** "Router beats aggressive by 2.94 bp" is about three times the whole real cost of crossing a 10-year; "p50 0.2 µs" times a constant-arithmetic handler at the resolution of the Windows clock.
5. **The public face contradicts the repo.** The site home page still says "Phase 0: foundations only", and governance documents describe reviews and labels that have never happened.

None of this is hidden dishonesty: most of it is disclosed somewhere in 9,000 lines of documentation. The problem is that the headline rows say one thing and the caveats say another, and a sceptical reader stops at the headline.

## 2. Profit and research evidence

### 2.1 The loss is an identity [C]

- Desk half-spread clamp: 0.02 to 0.08 bp (`rates-app/.../loop/DeskSettings.java:59-60`).
- Competitor median half-spread: 0.25 bp on swaps, 1/128 on bonds (`rates-venues/.../presets.json:10`).
- Informed flow: 20% of RFQs, each moving the mid 4 competitor half-spreads against the dealer after 30 s (`presets.json:13-15`, `D2cRfqSimulator.java:556-561`).
- Expected adverse cost on a swap trade: 0.2 × 4 × 0.25 = 0.20 bp. Maximum earned: 0.08 bp. Hedging costs a further ~0.09 bp.

Ledger seq 25, central band, per 15-minute session on 344,584 USD of client DV01 [R]:

| Line | USD | bp of client DV01 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Spread capture | +45.0k | +0.131 |
| Inventory marking | −124.0k | −0.360 |
| Hedge cost | −29.5k | −0.086 |
| Fees | −0.5k | −0.002 |
| Total | −109.0k | −0.316 |

Inventory marking stays at −0.35 to −0.40 bp per DV01 in every variant tested, whatever the spread. It is the injected jump, not inventory management. With the expected best competitor quote near 0.16 bp and break-even near 0.36 bp plus hedging, no dealer who wins at best price can make money in this simulator.

### 2.2 The simulator cannot reward hedging [C for the mechanism, U empirically]

- Each instrument's mid is an independent random walk (`D2cRfqSimulator.java:160-168`). There is no common rate factor, so a Treasury hedge does not offset a swap position.
- Each increment is rounded to the tick on every poll. At one-second polling a bond's step has sd ≈ 0.001 points against a 1/128 tick, so it needs a ~3.9 sigma draw to move. Realised bond volatility is near zero and depends on the polling interval.
- The desk hedges ~99% of client DV01 in instruments that neither move nor correlate, and pays ~29k a session for no risk reduction.

### 2.3 The positive results [R]

- `model_spread` earns 2.22mm a day by quoting ~0.84 bp against a 0.25 bp competitor median. The default outcome model lets a quote 0.6 bp behind the best still win ~15% of the time.
- The hit-rate model's out-of-sample log-loss is circular: its labels are simulated from the same declared logistic.
- V1H-001 (hedge slicing, +300k) and R2B-001 (netting) are simulator tautologies: our orders have no impact and the touch refills at once, so touch-sized children are nearly free.
- The router benchmark is covered in section 5.

### 2.4 Statistics and ledger [R]

- n = 20 sessions with session sd ~157k cannot resolve 30k effects. Two sessions exceed −400k. The five "holdout" sessions (+72k) are carried by one session.
- The three assumption-band points share sessions, so "at every band point" is one observation.
- The registered test is an unpaired Welch on a paired design, so every Holm p is 1; the narrative then leans on unregistered paired intervals.
- Looks are counted per pre-registration id. AUDIT-11 to 14 and AUDIT-21 to 25 are at least nine reads of the same 20 sessions, each recorded as look 1.
- No correction across the ledger's 32 experiments. The chain has no external anchor and was rewritten at a merge; CI verifies only the forward ledger.
- Honest point: pre-registrations really are committed before results (36 s to 12 min ahead).

### 2.5 The forward log will read as a track record [C]

`ForwardReport.java:104` prints `mean / sd × √252` after 60 sessions on synthetic spread income plus the real overnight mark. With intraday moves off, the synthetic part is near-deterministic income, so the ratio will be very large. The only real component, residual DV01 times the overnight move, has mean zero and measures no skill.

### 2.6 What would raise P&L for sound reasons

1. One simulator with a factor model for mids (level, slope, curvature fitted to the committed par-curve history), unrounded latent mid, swap-spread and futures-basis factors.
2. Calibrate adverse selection so competitors earn zero profit at the cover, using the DTCC swap prints already downloaded (intraday volatility, dispersion around a running mid, signed post-trade drift by size).
3. Make best-price clients the headline case; keep the logistic as a sensitivity. Remove the Sharpe table and the forward ratio.
4. Price from break-even: adverse cost by tier and size, plus actual hedge cost, plus inventory risk. Section 3 has the mechanics.
5. Run thousands of full-day sessions, register paired tests as primary, count looks by journal hash, and apply a false-discovery correction across the ledger.

**Design changes with the highest value**

1. Cover-relative pricing: P(win | distance to predicted cover by tenor, size, dealer count), with cover-gap feedback wired.
2. Fitted three-factor covariance; hedge level fully, then slope.
3. One inventory reservation price from ladder plus working hedges, capped at the true hedge cost; a real size curve.
4. Persisted multi-horizon client markouts feeding tier and side-specific skew.
5. Internalise first inside a variance budget with a time stop; hedge passively and cross only on breach; futures as a hedge with a basis limit.
6. Quote protection: pull at about half the quoted distance, price check at acceptance, time-to-live by volatility.
7. Limits sized to P&L: daily loss around 3 to 5 times expected daily income, per-client and per-RFQ DV01, collar, fat-finger.

## 5. Smart order router and market data

### 5.1 The 2.94 bp claim [R]

- One tick on the 10-year is 1.59 bp of price. A 2.94 bp saving is 1.85 ticks, about 290 USD per 1mm, roughly three times the real cost of crossing.
- The baselines must fire the whole parent as one IOC at t=0 into touch depth of 12, 6 and 4 lots, against parents of 10 to 60 lots. The router gets ~60 s and five slices with books that refill.
- The even split forces a third of the order onto a venue that is always a tick worse.
- The router's queue model is given the simulator's own generating parameters. Other participants' orders have no price impact; only ours do.
- Mean cost only; the router's ~75 s of price risk is not charged.
- In the wired process the router is given one venue (`DeskGroup.java:95`), so it never routes.

This row should be relabelled as a self-consistency check or withdrawn until there are fair baselines.

## 8. What is good

- Deterministic replay to an equal hash, with latched clock and journaled timers; hash-chained segments; frame length published last.
- Allocation-free loop, router and FIX codec, with ArchUnit hot-path rules proven to fail on violating fixtures.
- Analytics: schedules, day counts and compounding conventions ported carefully; 224 QuantLib goldens; all 84 note and bond and 332 bill auctions of 2024 reproduced; correct adjoint gradients.
- Child-order accounting: ExecID dedupe, late fills after cancel booked, quantity re-routed only after venue confirmation.
- Risk engine reservation maths is careful and overflow-safe; flatten-only mode after a daily-loss kill is the right design.
- Post-trade: BigDecimal weighted-average cost, versioned trades, duplicate and conflict audit.
- Research discipline: pre-registrations committed before results; failures recorded as failures; no sign error or double count found in attribution.
- The safepoint diagnosis of the journaling tail is real engineering.
- `-Werror`, Error Prone, SpotBugs; nine required checks on Linux and Windows.

### (a) Corrections to the review

- **TY deliverable window (A11, Section 10).** The review recalled a window up to 10 years and wondered about a cap at 96 months. CBOT Rule 19101.A, as amended by CBOT Submission 25-099 for contract months from March 2026, admits notes with a remaining term of at least 6 years 6 months and less than 8 years: at most 95 whole months. A note with exactly 8 years remaining is not deliverable (`TreasuryFutureContract.TY`, `FuturesTest`). The 2-year tick the review flagged is indeed 1/8 of 1/32, 0.00390625 (CME SER-8171).
- **Price collar and fat-finger checks (Q9).** The review said there was no collar or fat-finger check. `RiskEngine.sizeAndPrice` already enforced a price collar, a fat-finger quantity and a fat-finger price fraction before the fix round. What was missing is configuration: the desk settings set neither a collar nor a fat-finger quantity, so both were unlimited, while the fat-finger price check (10% from fair, the `RiskLimits` default) was on.
- **Empty risk buckets (A4).** The review said the 1, 3 and 7-year buckets are empty by construction. Only the 1-year bucket is exactly empty. Each pillar sits at its swap's last payment date, a few days past the tenor, and is split linearly onto the neighbouring tenors, so the 3-year and 7-year buckets hold a small spill of the 2-year and 5-year pillars (asserted below 0.2% of gross DV01; 0.10% and 0.08% on the fix round's run of `PricingRobustnessTest`, printed, not asserted).
- **The 12-year pillar (Section 10).** It does receive curve inputs: `CurveFeed` sends a ParSwap input for every swap template of the universe, the 12-year (template 2005) included, and the 12-year pillar feeds the 20-year bucket.
- **The "296 bytes" allocation failure (E5).** The 296-byte loop allocation failure on the Linux runner is a 2026-10-31 event fixed in pull request 2 (DR-INT-02, LEARN.md Section 13). The fix round reported that it did not find it among the recent CI failures it read; the allocation part of E5 is therefore not confirmed as a current cause of flaky runs. This documentation pass did not re-read the CI logs.
